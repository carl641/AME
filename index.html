Two alloys, both chosen for propulsion.

We run the materials our customers actually fly, and we know how each one behaves in the build.

IN718

Inconel 718

A nickel superalloy that keeps its strength and creep resistance where aluminium and steel give up. The default for turbine, manifold and structural hardware that runs hot.

  • High temperature strength
  • Creep resistant
  • Weldable

GRCop-42

GRCop-42

A copper chromium niobium alloy developed by NASA. It moves heat like copper while holding strength past 600 °C, which is why it lines regeneratively cooled combustion chambers.

  • High thermal conductivity
  • Strength above 600 °C
  • NASA developed

How a model becomes a part.

Five moves. You hear from an engineer at every one of them, not a portal.

  1. Quote

    Send a model and a material. You get pricing and an honest read on whether the geometry will build.

  2. Review

    We flag overhangs, trapped powder and thin walls that will not survive, then propose the fix.

  3. Build

    Fused layer by layer on the M400, monitored through the run.

  4. Finish

    Stress relief, support removal and surface work to your callout.

  5. Inspect and ship

    Dimensional checks and build documentation travel with the parts.

A small shop, sat next to the people who launch things.

Additive Manufacturing & Engineering was incorporated in 2018 in Huntsville, Alabama, a few miles from Marshall Space Flight Center. We are a HUBZone and Small Disadvantaged Business certified supplier, and the team behind the machines has 35 combined years in additive.

Customers come to us for intricate metal parts, prototypes and models that conventional manufacturing cannot produce, and for pilot series work in medical, automotive and aerospace.
